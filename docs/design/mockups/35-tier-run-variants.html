MOCK-35 — F variants, current tier outranking the links

Four ways to make the tier you are on the loudest thing in the row while the other tiers stay tappable. Brand is reduced to a small chevron in all four, so no inactive tier outranks the active one. Three tiers first, since that is where the app is heading.

MOCK-35-F-1
Weight, not colour

The minimum change that fixes the fault. Current tier at body size in semibold ink; other tiers drop to small muted text with a brand chevron. Same shape as the F you saw, with the colour hierarchy inverted.

MOCK-35-F-2
The current tier carries the word count

Your suggestion. The active tier and its word count bind into one ink lockup at the left, and the other tiers move to the right as quiet links. The count stops being a loose fact in the row and becomes part of the statement: this tier, this many words.

The clearest reading of the four. Binding the count to the tier gives the active item more mass than any other element without enlarging anything, and pushing the links right restores the left-to-right order of importance — where you are, then where you could go. It also degrades best: at two tiers there is one link, at four there are three, and the left half never changes.
MOCK-35-F-3
The current tier on a raised chip

Surface instead of type: the active tier sits on a parchment chip with a hairline; the others stay flat links. The strongest separation of the four.

Strongest, and the one with a real objection. An outlined container around the selected item of a horizontal row, sitting directly above the subtabs, is the selected-state language DES-28 explicitly kept out of this region — .pill--on and .tab--on already own it. Worth seeing on device, but if it reads as a segmented control, that is the objection landing rather than a matter of taste.
MOCK-35-F-4
The current tier in the display face

The active tier is set in the serif display face at lead size; the links stay in the UI face, small and muted. The contrast is typographic rather than chromatic — the same device the app already uses to separate titles from metadata.

Note
What none of these decide

The in-path behaviour is unchanged in all four — other tiers greyed, not offered, per DES-29 — and the suppressed upward cross-reference at the foot is a separate decision already settled. If a tier 3 is ever authored without a matching Advanced path, the greyed-out label is what a learner sees, and that is worth a second look when the time comes.
