1 · Drawer — the current pattern, restyled

Same five destinations, same settings section. Emoji icons replaced with stroke icons to match the rest of the system — a fixed set of five is exactly where real icons beat emoji. Active state uses the 3px edge rule, the same emphasis device as everywhere else.

2 · Bottom tab bar — the alternative

Five destinations is right at the limit for a tab bar, and it's the standard mobile pattern: always visible, thumb-reachable, no hidden menu. The descriptions ("Curated curriculum, ordered & tracked") don't survive the move — they'd live on each destination's own screen instead.

3 · The collision, and the fix

A tab bar plus the docked action bar is 118px of permanent bottom chrome. On an 812px phone that's 15% of the screen gone before any content — and two competing "what do I do now" zones stacked on each other.

The fix stated plainly

The action bar replaces the tab bar; they never coexist. Global nav belongs at top-level destinations. Once you're inside a lesson, the useful action isn't "go to Translate" — it's finish this and continue. Swapping one 58px bar for one 60px bar costs nothing and removes the competing-zones problem entirely.

Getting out is already handled: the contextual row's back target sits at the top, labelled with its destination. So a topic screen has exactly one way out (top) and one way forward (bottom), which is cleaner than having both plus a tab bar.

Drawer or tabs?

I'd take tabs. Five destinations behind a hamburger means Review and Translate are effectively invisible — Review in particular is the feature that brings someone back, and it currently carries a badge nobody sees unless they open the menu. A tab bar puts that badge permanently in view, which is worth more than the 58px it costs.

The counter-argument is real though: the drawer's descriptions do genuine teaching work for a five-destination app where "Topics" and "Learning Path" aren't obviously different things. If you keep the drawer, A over B — the Oxblood panel in B is what your tokens currently produce, and it's a lot of deep red at full height, with the nested tint-on-tint active state fighting the ground.

Not either/or: tabs for the five destinations, and the header's menu icon becomes a settings sheet for audio speed and anything later (voice toggle, account, subscription). That keeps the header's right corner useful and gives settings somewhere to grow — which matters if this becomes a paid product with an account to manage.

Deferred deliberately

The drawer currently resolves its own active state with (state.checkpoint || state.fromPath) ? 'path' : state.nav — so a topic opened from a path lights "Learning Path" rather than "Topics". That logic is correct and carries over to tabs unchanged; worth not losing it in a rewrite.
