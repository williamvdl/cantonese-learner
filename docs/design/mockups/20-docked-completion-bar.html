Now that the bar is permanent and 58px of the screen bottom is already spoken for, the question is not just docked or in-flow but how much more of the bottom edge the completion action can reasonably take.

The scope is narrower than it looks. The continuation only renders when a topic is opened from the learning path — getPathContext() returns null for a standalone topic and the whole element is omitted. So nothing below appears on Topics, Review, Translate, the dashboard, or a topic opened directly. It is a path-lesson-only surface, which is what makes spending bottom-edge space on it defensible.

52px, not 60. A 36px pill with 8px of padding — the button drops under --tap-min but the bar is the target, so the effective hit area is the full 52px. Shortened to “Mark complete”: the word lesson was doing no work in a bar that only ever appears inside one.

The four states, docked and slim

The continuation has four states today and the docked bar must carry all of them, not just the first. This is where the slim form earns itself — three of the four are not a button at all.

1 · not complete

Ghost, not filled — a jade outline. The quiz has its own filled action, and §3.1 allows exactly one; this is the same reasoning that already makes the built card's button a ghost.

2 · complete, next step

Done

Done state collapses to one word and a tick on the left; the forward step takes the rest. The card spends a full row on “Lesson complete” — at 52px that row has to earn its place, and it does not.

3 · complete, next is a checkpoint

Done

Same row, milestone diamond instead of a numbered node — the substitution the built card already makes, carried over unchanged.

4 · complete, path finished

Beginner path complete

No forward action exists, so the bar is a statement rather than a control. The card's two-line celebration does not fit and should not try — a path ending is worth more than a 52px strip, so the fuller treatment stays at the foot of the lesson and the bar just states the fact.

B2 saves 8px against the original, not 20. Worth saying plainly: the slim form helps, but it does not make the stack cheap. The honest comparison is 110px against 58px — the docked bar nearly doubles the bottom chrome inside path lessons, and the reason to accept that is that the completion action is currently invisible, which is a worse problem than a shorter lesson viewport.

D's 34px is below the tap minimum, and that is the whole objection. .tab sets min-height: var(--tap-min); reaching 34px means overriding it downward on the app's five primary navigation targets. This project has spent four phases removing sub-44px controls and standing check 2 reported zero min-height misses for the first time at v117 — D reintroduces five, at the bottom edge of the screen where thumbs are least precise. The 24px it saves is real; so is that.

The second cost is that navigation changes shape. The tab bar would be 58px with labels everywhere in the app and 34px without them inside path lessons — the surface you rely on to be constant becomes the one that moves. Icon-only tabs are also the state in which a learner has to already know what the five glyphs mean, and Path, Topics and Review are not self-evident icons.

What the bar does not carry: path progress. It only ever appears in a path lesson, and the context row plus stage stepper at the top of that same screen already show position — putting it in the bar too would be the same fact twice on one screen, which §3.4 exists to prevent.

Recommendation

B2 over D, though D is closer than I expected. Dropping the labels is the single biggest saving available — 24px, more than slimming the bar itself gave — and if the stack proves too heavy in use, D is the first thing to reach for. But it buys that by putting five primary navigation targets under the tap minimum and by making the main menu change shape depending on where you are, and I would not spend either of those before knowing the 110px stack is actually a problem. Ship B2, judge it, and hold D as the known lever — which is what §3.10 said all along, just for a collision that had not happened yet.

B2 — docked, slim, 52px, all four states. The in-flow card is retired rather than kept alongside: two routes to the same action in the same screen is how the old top action zone and the card came to coexist in the first place. State 4 is the one exception, and deliberately so — the path-complete celebration stays in-flow, because a path ending deserves more than a strip and the bar has no forward action to offer there anyway.

Reversibility is the argument for building rather than deciding. The bar is one fixed element and a wrapper class; if 110px proves too much in use, removing it restores the card exactly, since both render from the same four states. That is a genuinely cheap experiment, which is the case for doing it your way rather than settling it on paper.
