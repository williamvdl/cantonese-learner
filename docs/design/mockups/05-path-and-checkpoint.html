The structural change: one state system, three colours

Checkpoints currently use CP_GOLD = '#B7861E', hardcoded in render.js and sitting outside the palette entirely — a fourth accent alongside brand orange, the blue, and the completion green. It's retired here in favour of three states that mean something and are reused everywhere:

Current — the thing to do next
Done
Milestone — checkpoints

Milestone reuses the Oxblood header colour, which is what makes a checkpoint read as an event rather than just another card: it's the only place in the body of the app where the header colour reappears.

Other decisions

  • Checkpoints keep the diamond, but as a rotated square with the border language rather than a text glyph — crisper at small sizes, and it scales to the 44px hero mark without going fuzzy.
  • Topic emoji dropped from the step rows. The numbered rail already does the wayfinding, and 41 emoji down a scrolling list was a lot of noise. They're still fine on the dashboard topic grid, where they genuinely aid scanning.
  • Stage bands are now a labelled rule rather than a filled band — same treatment as the section labels on Topic/Learn, so the two screens share a language.
  • The hub's activities are numbered, which makes the "suggested flow: recall → produce" hint redundant as prose but still useful as a label. Numbering also makes the third Stage 3 activity slot in without redesign.
  • Finish button is a disabled ghost until the checkpoint completes — the old dim class did this by opacity, which muddied the text colour.

Worth your eye

  • Completed checkpoints drop the Oxblood tint and go quiet white with a green mark. Milestone colour signals "this is ahead of you"; once done, it stops competing. Scroll the left screen to Stage 1 to see both.
  • The dashed third-activity slot is annotation, not a proposed UI element — it's there to show the layout accommodates Stage 3.
