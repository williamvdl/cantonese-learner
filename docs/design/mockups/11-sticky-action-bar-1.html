1 · Live — scroll it, and toggle the state

The bar is docked to the phone, not the page: it floats over content on a blurred parchment ground and never moves. Scroll the frame to see content pass beneath it. "3 of 5" now sits in the contextual row's meta slot — C's framing with none of C's extra band.

2 · What "the bottom" means on each tab

The bar belongs to the topic shell, not to any tab's content — completion is a property of the lesson, not of the thing you happened to be doing. But it has to yield when a tab owns the bottom of the screen itself.

Answering "what is the bottom?"

Tab / stateBarWhy
LearnShownNo competing action. Content gets bottom padding so the tone guide clears it.
ChatShownSame — the conversation has no terminal CTA of its own.
Quiz · mid-questionHiddenThe quiz owns the bottom. Two primary buttons stacked is the failure mode.
Quiz · resultMergedThe result screen's own CTA becomes the continuation — one button, not three.
Standalone (no path)HiddenContinuation is a path concept. Nothing to continue to.

One slot, two states

The bar is a single persistent slot that swaps content rather than two different components: ghost "Mark complete" before, then tick plus a filled "Next" after. That keeps exactly one filled button on screen at any moment, which is the rule the rest of the system already follows. When the next item is a checkpoint the fill turns Oxblood and takes the diamond, so the milestone reads before you've even tapped it.

Why not hide it until you scroll

I tried a scroll-threshold reveal and dropped it: on a short topic (10 words, no sentences) the threshold never fires, and on a long one the bar appearing mid-scroll pulls the eye away from the content you're reading. Always-present is more predictable, and 60px on an 812px screen is a fair trade for never having to hunt for it.

The question underneath this one

Worth deciding separately: should passing the quiz mark the lesson complete automatically? The app already knows the score, and manual marking is the kind of admin that a paid product would quietly do for you. It'd make the bar's first state rare rather than routine. I've left the manual toggle in because changing it is a behaviour decision, not a design one — but it's the sort of thing worth resolving before the refactor rather than after.
