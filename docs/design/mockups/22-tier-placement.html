Tier placement, and what a tier switch actually means

Mocked on v121. Two questions that turn out to be one: where the tier control sits, and what it does when you arrived from a path. The second answers the first. The nameplate reads 廣東話 (gwong2 dung1 waa2).

0 · What the data says — this decides most of it

Before drawing anything, I counted. Eight of the 42 topics have more than one tier, so for 34 topics this control does not render at all (renderRoundSelector() returns '' below two rounds) and the screen has a second, shorter shape that has never been mocked. More importantly:

No topic appears twice in one path — not once, in 52 path lessons. Every tier of every topic belongs to exactly one path, and for all eight multi-tier topics tier 1 is Beginner and tier 2 is Intermediate. So your question — "does a tier switch mean they've jumped to the path it's part of, or just the topic?" — has a factual answer rather than a design answer: switching tier is always crossing paths. There is no same-path tier switch in the data, and nothing in the app creates one. The tier pills are not a filter on a topic; they are a doorway between two paths wearing a filter's clothes.

Found while measuring — this is a live defect, not a design question

Switching tier inside a path lesson leaves the chrome describing the tier you left, and the completion action writes to the wrong lesson. The handler at [data-round] sets state.currentRound and nothing else. state.fromPathTier and state.activePath are untouched, and getPathContext() reads state.fromPathTier || state.currentRound.

So: open Greetings from Beginner, tap Tier 2. The words on screen are the Intermediate path's tier-2 lesson. The context row still says First Words · 2 of 5 · Beginner. The stepper still highlights Greetings as Beginner step 2. The docked bar still offers Next in First Words → Yes/No & Politeness. And tapping "mark complete" calls toggleLessonComplete('beginner', 'greetings', 1) — it marks the tier-1 Beginner lesson you are no longer looking at, while the Intermediate lesson you just read stays unstarted.

Every option below fixes this, because every option below stops the two states disagreeing. Doing nothing is the only choice that does not.

1 · What a tier switch should mean, in path context

Taking this first, because the answer changes what the control can be. Four ways to resolve it.

BehaviourChrome after the tapVerdict
NowContent changes, path state does notDescribes the tier you leftdefect
FFollow — re-enter as a lesson of the owning pathCorrect: Intermediate stage, Intermediate siblingshonest but abrupt
LLock — no tier control in path context at allUnchanged, nothing to disagreehides content
XCross-reference — tier is plain text at the top; the other tier is offered at the foot, named, as a deliberate crossingCorrect, and the crossing is stated before it happenspick

Why not F. Following is honest — the chrome would be right and the completion write would be right — but a segmented pill is the wrong affordance for it. A two-up toggle says these are two views of the thing you are looking at. It does not say this one is in a different course, and taking it moves your place there. A control that quietly relocates you between paths on a single tap, from a position that looks like a filter, is the same class of surprise as the current bug, just with correct state behind it.

Why not L. Locking removes the disagreement by removing the door, and for eight topics that means the tier-2 content is reachable only via Topics or the Intermediate path. It is defensible and it is the cheapest fix — but it answers "is this clear to the user?" by making sure they never encounter it, and the connection between a topic's two tiers is genuinely useful information.

X keeps the door and moves it to where doors already are. The foot of the lesson is already the path action zone (MOCK-10-cont, DES-22), so a forward move that lands somewhere else belongs there, not in the header. The top of the screen goes back to stating one fact — which tier you are in — and stops offering a control at all. That also fixes the placement problem for free: with no toggle to house, the tier is just text, and text fits under the title.

The crossing is named before it is taken — which path, which tier, how much. It sits above the docked bar and is deliberately quieter: outline node, muted arrow, no brand fill. The path's own forward step keeps the brand. Only rendered for the eight topics that have a second tier; absent for the other 34.

Taking the crossing calls openPathLesson() with the owning path. It is the same function the timeline steps and the stepper siblings already use, so nothing new resets state — which also means state.activePath, state.fromPathTier and the chrome all move together by construction, and the mark-complete write lands on the lesson you are actually reading. The defect closes as a side effect of the navigation being real navigation.

3 · From Topics — where a real toggle still makes sense

Opened from Topics there is no path context to contradict, and no completion write to land on the wrong lesson. A tier switch here is what it looks like: two rounds of one topic. So the control stays — but as the meta line under the title, not a pill row above it. Same line, same position, same type as option X's static version; the difference is that the tiers are live.

One thing I could not settle from the data, and would not guess at. The cross-reference assumes a learner on the Beginner path should be able to walk into an Intermediate lesson from inside a topic. Nothing in the app is locked ("Tap any to jump in — nothing is locked"), so it is consistent — but it is also the only place where one path offers a door into another, and it does so at the exact moment someone is mid-way through the first. Option L (no door at all) is the honest alternative if that is not wanted, and it is strictly less work. This is the choice worth making deliberately rather than inheriting from the mockup.
