MOCK-32 — making the tier legible

The tier ladder works; it is just quiet. “Tier 1 · 10 words” is 12px muted text under a 26px serif title, so the one fact that tells you which lesson you are in is the smallest thing on the screen. Three ways to give it weight without touching the architecture.

MOCK-32-A
Same line, more weight

The tier number rises to body size in full ink while the rest of the line stays as it is. Nothing moves, nothing is added, and no class is created — two declarations on .tier-state. The tier stops being the smallest text on screen but stays in a meta line, which is still where the eye goes last.

MOCK-32-B
Tier as an eyebrow above the title

The tier moves above the lesson name as a muted eyebrow, reusing the shipped .eyebrow primitive with a colour modifier. It reads top-down: which tier, then which lesson, then how many words. The word count stays in the meta line with the ladder, so the line keeps its job and loses its crowding.

MOCK-32-C
Tier in the title lockup

The tier sits beside the lesson name in the display face, baseline-aligned. Strongest reading of the three because the tier is inside the title rather than beneath it. The risk is that it competes with the lesson name for the same glance, and on a long title such as “Numbers & Counting” it wraps to a second line.
