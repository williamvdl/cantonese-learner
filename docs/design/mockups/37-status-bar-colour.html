Status bar colour

The status bar is the strip at the top of the phone showing the time and battery. An installed web app picks its colour through theme-color. Today it is #1A1815, which isn't a token, above the oxblood header. Mockup 36 drew it in oxblood, and on device-sized frames that made the nameplate look low. This page compares five colours from the palette, all on the approved MOCK-36-pair nameplate.

Solid gold = the nameplate's centre. Dashed blue = the middle of the block the eye reads as one colour.

Why oxblood looks off centre. The status bar sits outside the app, so the header can only centre the nameplate on its own 68px. When the bar is the same colour, the eye reads one 92px block instead, and the nameplate sits about 12px below that block's middle. Any colour that reads as different from the header puts the two lines back together. Turn the lines on to see it.

Five options

MOCK-37-oxblood · the same as the header

9:41

Good evening

--header-bg #6E2639. The bar and the header become one 92px block, but the nameplate is still centred on the 68px header, so it sits about 12px below the block's middle. That is the effect you noticed. The only fix inside this option is unequal header padding, which would then look wrong anywhere the bar isn't oxblood, such as in a browser tab.

MOCK-37-deep · a darker oxblood recommended

9:41

Good evening

#481925, a darker shade of --header-bg. New value, as --header-deep. The same colour family, one step darker. The change in tone is enough for the eye to separate the bar from the header, so the nameplate reads as centred again, and the top of the screen still reads as one family of colour. The cost is one new token: a shade of an existing colour rather than a new hue, the way --brand-dark relates to --brand.

MOCK-37-ink · black

9:41

Good evening

--ink #1C1917. Effectively what you have today. Today's #1A1815 isn't a token, but it is visually identical to --ink, so choosing this just points it at the token. It gives the strongest, most neutral separation and is how most apps look. The only cost is that it adds a colour to the chrome that the header itself doesn't use.

MOCK-37-brand · brand orange not recommended

9:41

Good evening

--brand #C2410C. It separates clearly, but it becomes the loudest thing on the screen, above everything. §4 gives --brand one meaning, the thing to do next, and spending it on permanent chrome weakens that meaning everywhere else. Orange against oxblood is also the least calm pairing here.

MOCK-37-paper · parchment not recommended

9:41

Good evening

--parchment-deep #F0EBE4, with dark icons. The app's paper colour, so the header reads as a band laid across the page. It separates strongly, but a light strip above a dark header looks upside down, and it becomes a bright bar on an OLED screen at night.

What Android does with the icons. The time and battery icons switch between light and dark on their own, based on how light the chosen colour is. Only MOCK-37-paper gets dark icons. Nothing else about the header changes in any option.
