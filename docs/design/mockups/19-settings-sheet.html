Settings sheet

P6-4. The nameplate reads 廣東話 (gwong2 dung1 waa2). Mockup 13 put a settings cog in the header corner where the hamburger sits today, but never drew what opens. This does. Three placements, drawn behind the v116 header and the P6-2 tab bar, so the collisions are visible rather than assumed.

What actually goes in it

Two settings exist in the whole app, and the second is currently in the wrong place:

SettingLives todayNote
Audio speedThe drawer's Settings sectionThe only live speed control — .drawer-speed-btn is the sole writer of state.speed. This is why P6-2 cannot ship before P6-4: retiring the drawer without this leaves a live setting with no route to it.
Gemini API keyInside Translate — the entry form when no key is set, and a “Reset API key” button in the screen footer once one isA setting living in a feature screen, which DES-11 says should not happen: settings live behind the header corner regardless of feature count. Moving it here is not scope creep; it is the same decision being applied consistently.

The first-run key form should stay in Translate. It is not a setting at that moment, it is a blocker on the feature — you cannot translate without it and the screen has nothing else to show. What moves here is changing or clearing a key you already have, which today is a bare button under the translation UI with no obvious reason to be there.

Where this ends up once DES-11's account and subscription arrive — those need room, and a sheet holding a billing history is the wrong shape. But with two rows it reads as a mistake: a whole screen, its own header, its own back stack, for one segmented control and a button. Right answer, wrong year.

Recommendation

S-sheet, sized to its contents. It matches what is actually in it, it cannot be confused with the navigation drawer it replaces, and it costs the least to build. Migration is cheap and worth naming now: the rows are the same markup in all three placements — only the container differs. When account and subscription land, S-sheet becomes S-screen by changing the wrapper, not the contents. Nothing here has to be rebuilt to get there.

Do not put a tick or an active state on the cog. Settings is not a destination — that is DES-11's whole point, and it is why the cog sits in the header corner rather than becoming a sixth tab. The cog lights while the sheet is open because it is the sheet's origin, not because settings is somewhere you now are.

Both dismissals need a history entry, the same as the drawer's: opening pushes, the scrim and the × both step back. Phone BACK closing the sheet rather than leaving the screen underneath is the behaviour to check on device.
