Jyut — the nameplate and the app icon

The naming direction is Jyut, from 粵 (jyut6), the character that means Cantonese itself, as in 粵語 (jyut6 jyu5). The purpose goes in a descriptor, Learn Cantonese, rather than into a compound name. This page draws the name in the two places it lives: the header nameplate, on every screen, and the home-screen icon with its label.

It replaces the placeholder 廣東話 (gwong2 dung1 waa2) / Cantonese Learner and the navy-and-gold 廣 (gwong2) icon, which predates the oxblood system and uses none of its colours. Nothing here is settled until it is approved; the naming decision and whichever options are picked then get one DES row each in DESIGN_DECISIONS.md.

Lowercase follows how jyutping is written (jyut6), and gives the wordmark two descenders. The safe zone is the circle Android guarantees to keep; everything outside it may be cropped. The status bar toggle shows today's #1A1815 against the proposed oxblood.

Part 1 · The nameplate

All three keep §2's three-slot header and DES-18's hugging nameplate button. Only the contents of the button change. Info and settings sit in the corners exactly as today.

MOCK-36-word · wordmark over descriptor

9:41●●●

The same structure as today, with the Latin name leading, which suits an English-speaking audience. It's the cleanest of the three. The drawback is that the header then carries no Chinese at all, and the link between the name and 粵 (jyut6) lives only in the icon.

MOCK-36-pair · character and reading, side by side recommended

9:41●●●

The only option where the brand explains itself: the character, then how it is read, which is exactly the pattern every other screen of the app teaches. It also ties the header to the icon on the home screen. Measured, it is no wider than MOCK-36-word: both plates are 110px, because the descriptor line is the widest thing in either.

MOCK-36-stack · character over name, no descriptor not recommended

9:41●●●

Today's exact two-line structure with new contents, so it's the smallest change. Drawn because it's the obvious minimal move, but it drops the descriptor, which you wanted in the header, and the name shrinks to a 9px label beneath a character most learners can't yet read. It also measures 30px wide, which puts the route home under the 44px --tap-min.

One rule needs a ruling for MOCK-36-pair. The standing rule is that every Chinese character is followed immediately by its jyutping in brackets. The pair puts the reading beside the character, but as Jyut, without brackets or the tone number. My proposal is to treat the nameplate as a wordmark rather than content, record that as the one exception in the same DES row, and keep the rule absolute everywhere else. 粵 (jyut6) in the header would read as a lesson rather than a name.

Part 2 · The app icon

Each option is drawn at the three shapes launchers cut it to: the circle your Pixel uses, the rounded square iOS and Samsung use, and the full square, which shows the whole canvas. Turn the safe zone on. The manifest marks the icon maskable, so only the dashed circle is guaranteed to survive.

Today · for comparison

On the circle, the gold frame loses its corners and survives only as four disconnected lines along the sides. That is what your Pixel shows today. The frame sits at 6% from the edge, while the safe zone starts at 10%.

MOCK-36-plain · off-white 粵 (jyut6) on oxblood recommended

The header's own two colours, --header-bg and --header-text, with the glyph in Noto Serif TC, the face §1 reserves for Chinese. There is no frame, so no shape can cut anything off. Opening the app is continuous: an oxblood icon, an oxblood splash screen, then an oxblood header.

MOCK-36-rule · plain, with a brand rule beneath

The short burnt-orange rule borrows the app's emphasis language: the 2px --brand rule under an active tab. It adds the second brand colour, but the glyph has to shrink about 8% to make room, and at home-screen size the rule reads more like an underline than a signature.

MOCK-36-paper · oxblood 粵 (jyut6) on parchment not recommended

The inverse: the app's body colours rather than its header colours. It's calm and reads like ink on paper, but on a light wallpaper or in a light app drawer it loses its edge entirely. Try the light wallpaper below.

Part 3 · On the home screen, at real size

Everything at the size a phone draws it, around 54px, among neighbouring apps. The label under each icon is the manifest's short_name. Today it is 廣東話 (gwong2 dung1 waa2), characters an English-speaking learner can't read. It would become Jyut.
