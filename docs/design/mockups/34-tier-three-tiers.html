MOCK-34 — E and F at three tiers

Both front runners at a three-tier topic, in every position. No topic has three tiers yet — 32 have one, 10 have two — so this is about which option survives the corpus growing, not about anything you can open today.

Tier 2 of 3 reads “Tier 2 of 3 · 10 words | ← 1 3 →” — four numbers in eleven characters, three of them tier numbers meaning different things. Grouping the pair together is what causes it: the bare-number shortening was safe when the rungs were an inch away at the right edge, and is not once they are adjacent. E would need full “Tier 1” / “Tier 3” labels here, which pushes the row to roughly 300px and starts crowding at small widths. At two tiers E is clean; at three it is the weaker of the two.

This is where F earns its keep. “of 3” becomes redundant and disappears, so the row gets shorter as tiers are added rather than longer, and the middle position needs no special case at all — the current tier is simply the ink one. The objection also gets stronger: three items in a row with one emphasised is a segmented control by any other name, sitting directly above the subtabs, which is precisely the reading DES-28 set out to avoid. At two tiers that is arguable. At three it is harder to argue.
The trade, stated plainly
Neither option is better at both counts

E is cleaner at two tiers and awkward at three. F is unremarkable at two and clearly better at three, but at three it looks most like the chooser DES-28 ruled out. Since every topic today has one or two tiers, E is the safer choice for the app as it exists, and F is the better choice for the app as it might grow. That is a judgement about content plans, not about pixels.
